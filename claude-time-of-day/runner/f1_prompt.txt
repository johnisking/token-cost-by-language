Specification: parseDuration(input)

1. If input is not a string, throw a TypeError.
2. Leading and trailing whitespace is ignored.
3. After that, the string may start with one sign character, "+" or "-". The rest is the duration.
4. A duration is one or more components. A component is a number immediately followed by a unit, with no space between them.
5. A number is one or more digits, optionally followed by "." and one or more digits. Examples: "2", "007", "1.5". Not allowed: ".5", "1.", "1e3", "1_000".
6. Units are lowercase only: "d" (86400000 ms), "h" (3600000 ms), "m" (60000 ms), "s" (1000 ms), "ms" (1 ms).
7. Components must appear in this order: d, h, m, s, ms. Each unit may appear at most once. Units may be skipped.
8. Components may be separated by one or more spaces, or by nothing ("1h30m" and "1h 30m" are both valid). No space is allowed between the sign and the first component.
9. Special case: if the whole duration (after the optional sign) is a single number with no unit, the unit is seconds. "90" means 90 seconds. A number without a unit is not allowed anywhere else ("1h30" is invalid).
10. The result is a Number of milliseconds: the sum of all components, rounded to the nearest integer with halves rounded away from zero, and negated if the sign is "-". "-0.0005s" gives -1.
11. Anything that does not match these rules throws an Error whose message starts with "Invalid duration".
